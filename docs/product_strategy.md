# GEOAPP Product Strategy Blueprint

This document converts the strategy prompts in `apps/context/master.md` and `apps/context/rules.md` into a concrete product definition for GEOAPP / GEOECON WARS.

The goal is to move the product from a visually compelling simulator into a trusted scenario-intelligence and decision-support platform.

## 1. Core Transformation

### What real problem the product should solve

GEOAPP should help professionals answer one urgent question:

`If a geopolitical event escalates in country X, what should we watch, what gets hit first, and what action should we take now?`

This is not a news problem. It is a decision problem.

Users do not need another globe, map, or feed. They need a fast system that turns geopolitical noise into:

- likely business exposure
- explainable scenario outcomes
- prioritized watch items
- communication-ready outputs

### What decision it should help users make

GEOAPP should support decisions such as:

- whether to hedge a commodity, FX, or regional exposure
- whether to escalate internal monitoring on a country or corridor
- whether to shift sourcing, logistics, or inventory assumptions
- whether to brief executives that a situation is low risk, elevated, or urgent
- which of several scenarios is more dangerous for a business, portfolio, or region

### What outcome users should get in under 3 minutes

Within 3 minutes, a user should be able to:

1. choose a country and scenario
2. see deterministic market and spillover impacts
3. understand why the model produced that result
4. see live signals that support or challenge the scenario
5. generate a shareable brief or report
6. decide what to monitor, hedge, or communicate next

### Why users would keep coming back

Users return if GEOAPP becomes their fastest path to:

- morning geopolitical risk review
- weekly scenario comparison
- executive briefing preparation
- monitoring watchlists of countries, commodities, sectors, and trade routes
- replaying and refining scenarios as events evolve

### Why they would pay

Users pay when the product reliably saves:

- analyst time
- executive attention
- communication cycles
- avoidable exposure mistakes

They will not pay for spectacle. They will pay for:

- decision support
- trust
- speed
- explainability
- repeatable reporting

### Why it becomes more than a globe app

The globe should remain an interaction surface, not the product thesis.

The actual product becomes:

- a deterministic scenario engine
- a geopolitical signal relevance engine
- an exposure interpretation layer
- a reporting and briefing system
- a team workflow for monitoring and planning

## 2. Ideal Product Positioning

### Best product category

`Geopolitical scenario intelligence and market exposure planning platform`

### Best one-line positioning statement

`GEOAPP helps teams understand how geopolitical events could affect markets, supply chains, and exposure before those effects fully price in.`

### Best elevator pitch

`GEOAPP is an explainable scenario-planning platform for geopolitical and market risk. Users select a country and event type, compare deterministic scenario outcomes against live signals, and generate decision-ready briefings, alerts, and replayable reports in minutes.`

### Best why-now angle

The world has structurally higher geopolitical volatility, fragmented trade relationships, supply-chain concentration risk, sanctions risk, commodity sensitivity, and executive demand for faster scenario planning. Most teams still stitch together news, spreadsheets, and analyst judgment manually.

### Best 3 target audiences to start with

1. Global macro and multi-asset investors
2. Corporate strategy / risk / treasury teams in internationally exposed companies
3. Trade, supply-chain, and market intelligence professionals

### Best initial narrow wedge market

`Small and mid-sized investment teams and independent macro professionals who need scenario tools but cannot justify a large enterprise geopolitical intelligence platform.`

This wedge is strong because:

- willingness to pay exists
- solo and small-team workflows are clearer
- reporting value is immediate
- market and scenario outputs are easier to validate than broad enterprise collaboration

### Emotional and business value by audience

#### Investors and analysts

- Emotional value: speed, conviction, clarity

#### Corporate risk / treasury / strategy

- Emotional value: reduced ambiguity, better preparedness

#### Supply-chain and trade intelligence teams

- Emotional value: control over chaotic events

## 3. Value Proposition In Detail

### Strategic value

- Feature: scenario comparison workspace
- Trigger: compare 2 to 4 geopolitical scenarios on the same country or region
- Output: ranked scenarios by severity, confidence, affected markets, and watch factors
- Why it matters: strategy teams need structured options, not isolated event views

### Operational value

- Feature: watchlists and scenario alerts
- Trigger: user follows countries, corridors, sectors, or assets
- Output: alerts when live signals materially change relevance or confidence
- Why it matters: teams need to know when to escalate monitoring

### Financial value

- Feature: exposure impact summary
- Trigger: user links or manually enters exposures, regions, sectors, or asset watchlists
- Output: prioritized impacts by market, commodity, FX, sector, and scenario
- Why it matters: turns general geopolitics into financially actionable interpretation

### Communication and reporting value

- Feature: executive summary generator
- Trigger: user runs a scenario or compares multiple scenarios
- Output: concise memo with headline, key risks, evidence, confidence, and actions
- Why it matters: most teams lose time translating analysis into stakeholder-ready communication

### Educational and training value

- Feature: replay library and tabletop mode
- Trigger: user replays past crises or simulated escalation sequences
- Output: reusable training scenarios and decision walkthroughs
- Why it matters: makes the product useful outside immediate live-event windows

### Team collaboration value

- Feature: shared scenarios, comments, and organization watchlists
- Trigger: user saves or shares a scenario
- Output: persistent team workspace around specific geopolitical risks
- Why it matters: institutional memory and collaboration drive retention

### Executive value

- Feature: board-ready briefing export
- Trigger: analyst promotes a scenario or watchlist update
- Output: one-page summary with scenario, confidence, key exposures, and next actions
- Why it matters: executives do not want dashboards; they want decisions and implications

## 4. What a World-Class Version Looks Like

A world-class GEOAPP lets users:

- monitor countries, regions, corridors, commodities, and scenarios
- run deterministic geopolitical scenarios with transparent rules
- compare those scenarios against live signal evidence
- map scenarios to portfolio, business, or supply-chain exposure
- generate analyst, executive, and board-ready outputs instantly
- collaborate as teams with approvals, notes, and watchlists

It feels trustworthy because:

- every scenario has a rules version
- every live signal has provenance
- every score has explainability
- every export includes confidence and limitations
- every result can be replayed and audited

It feels habit-forming because:

- the user has a daily watchlist
- risk changes over time
- teams rely on recurring briefings and alerts
- scenarios compound into institutional knowledge

It is better than dashboards or news apps because:

- it interprets, not just displays
- it compares futures, not only presents current facts
- it creates reusable outputs
- it produces explainable decision support instead of raw feeds

It is defensible because:

- proprietary scenario design and workflow matter more than raw data access
- deterministic rules plus explainability create trust
- watchlists, team workflows, saved scenarios, and reporting create switching costs
- customer data and usage can inform stronger domain-specific scenario templates over time

Customers say `we need this` when it becomes their default workflow for:

- escalation monitoring
- scenario planning
- risk briefing
- exposure communication

## 8. Trust, Explainability, and Credibility

GEOAPP should make trust visible.

### Deterministic engine trust model

- every scenario uses explicit rule logic
- identical input and rules version should produce identical outputs
- scenario assumptions must be inspectable

### Rules versioning

- show the active rules version on every result
- allow replay against historic rules
- log when rules changed materially

### Source freshness

- show last updated time on observed and forecast layers
- indicate stale, fresh, or delayed states visibly

### Signal provenance

- every supporting signal should display source, time, link, and signal type
- do not show unattributed intelligence

### Explainability

- each result should answer:
  - what rule fired
  - what inputs mattered
  - what live signals support this
  - what confidence level applies

### Confidence indicators

- separate scenario severity from model confidence
- distinguish low-evidence from low-risk

### Auditability

- saved runs should be immutable
- exports should include a report id, timestamp, and rules version
- admins should be able to inspect signal refresh status and cache status

### Limitations disclosures

- make clear that the engine is a decision-support tool, not an oracle
- explicitly state when outputs are scenario-based rather than predictive

### How to avoid looking like hype

- avoid exaggerated war-game copy
- reduce purely cinematic elements in professional outputs
- surface evidence and assumptions before visual drama

### How to make customers comfortable paying

- price around time saved, clarity gained, and workflow integration
- make trust, output quality, and repeatability central to the premium experience

## 12. What To Remove Or De-Emphasize

Do not overemphasize:

- globe spectacle as the main pitch
- overly game-like copy and war-game aesthetics
- raw signal feeds without interpretation
- novelty simulations with weak real-world relevance
- premium gating that only restricts quantity, not workflow value

Avoid UX patterns that make it feel like a toy:

- leading with animation before evidence
- oversized visual effects without decision outputs
- ambiguous severity scores without explanation
- replay experiences that look cool but do not inform action

Avoid product and architecture decisions that slow the company down:

- building broad enterprise features too early
- overinvesting in white-label too early
- adding AI-generated narrative everywhere without trust controls
- creating too many unrelated scenario types before watchlists and reporting are strong
